## "My Awesome, Annotated Mixtape #6" Metadata Process

### The Data Collection 

Rather than simply using the official soundtracks for each of the films, I compiled my own, more comprehensive playlists in the order than the songs play. I did this by first looking through fan-sourced sites like <a href="https://www.tunefind.com/">Tunefind</a> and <a href="https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0118749/soundtrack/">IMDB</a>, then using those as guides while going through each film and checking for myself. This was the way I could be most sure of my data. 

### The Metadata

I used <a href="https://exportify.net/">Exportify</a> to obtain my metadata spreadsheet. It is a platform that accesses the Spotify Web API via your account log in. The metadata I recieved was therefore whatever Spotify had stored for themselves, and all the labels were dictated by whatever algorithms Spotify uses. I discuss this more in depth in the Method and Process section of my essay but there were some things I wanted to keep in mind (and make sure viewers kept in mind) regarding this metadata sourcing, namely the genre labels for each song, since genre is a subjective and complicated kind of categorization.

There are many metadata categories included on the sheet that I ended up not using, such as the many different quantitative scores measuring aspects such as popularity, acousticness, and energy. However, there are many ways that this sort of information could have been included in my project; I was particularly considering using a combination of the various technical "scores" to somehow graph the atmosphere of each film. 

The "diegetic?" column in the chart is the one category that I added myself; it was not a part of the Spotify metadata, as it pertains specifically to the songs as they are played in the films. "True" means that the song was played diegetically (in the world of the film). "False," on the other hand, means it was extradiegetic (only heard by the audience of the film). "Both," which only shows up a few of times, means that the song was transdiegetic (moved between being diegetic and extradiegetic, or vice versa, such as when a song on the radio suddenly becomes much louder and clearly extradiegetic soundtracking).
